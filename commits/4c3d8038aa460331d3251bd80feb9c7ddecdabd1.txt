feat(cameras): FPS and third-person game camera rigs (#51)
Splits the game cameras from the editor's orbit camera and adds the two rigs the
roadmap needs, with their math as a tested pure kernel.

- CameraRigMath (pure): yaw/pitch to forward/right/up, the ±89° pitch clamp, yaw
  wrapping, one look step (1 pixel = sensitivity radians) and the orbit eye
  position. Documents the engine's camera angle convention in one place.
- FpsCameraRig: eye at the rig's position (the character's head once #59 drives
  it), mouse look with the clamped pitch, optional pointer lock. OnUnload releases
  a lock it took.
- ThirdPersonCameraRig: orbits a pivot at a distance with an over-the-shoulder
  offset, mouse look and wheel zoom inside a distance range, optional pointer lock.
  Wall pull-in stays with #60, which only has to shorten Distance.
- The scene ticks its "Camera" components in DefaultScene.OnUpdated, after the
  window polled the input service and before the renderer reads the view matrix;
  the editor's orbit camera has a no-op OnUpdated there, so nothing is double-driven.
- Pointer lock is opt-in (LockPointer, default false): arming it by default would
  capture the cursor in a plain engine run, and #58 decides when a running game owns
  the pointer.
- The legacy Camera is deleted: with the two rigs there is one game camera set, and
  it threw NotImplementedException on two interface members (LSP) and produced NaN
  in Move when no direction was given. The demo scene now uses the FPS rig at the
  same vantage point, so the framing is unchanged.
- OrbitCamera is untouched behaviourally; only its doc comment (which named the
  deleted class) was updated.

Tests: 49 new (30 pure math, 19 rig) — basis at every yaw, the clamp at and past
±89°, yaw wrap properties, 1:1 look, orbit at distance/shoulder/pitch, the view
matrix basis, look/zoom from the input service, pointer lock request and release,
lifecycle no-ops, and NaN-free Move.

Closes #51
